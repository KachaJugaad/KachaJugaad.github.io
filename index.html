Vancouver Airport Authority  ·  2025–2026
Fleet intelligence where no decision system existed
Portfolio Governance
The problem

"Operators had the data. They just had no way to act on it. Every peak period was a negotiation between judgment calls and incomplete signals. The tools existed. The product didn't."

What I found

Maximo data was there, fragmented across systems. Operators were running airside fleet allocation on instinct during peaks — idle assets, missed signals, unclear who owned the call. No escalation model. No ownership clarity. The data was available; nobody had built the decision layer.

What I built

Spent time airside first, before writing a line of spec. Integrated AI agents with existing Maximo data. Defined a decision-support product, intake process, and SOPs. Built escalation paths. Aligned operators, vendors, and executives without adding a single new system or headcount.

−50% fleet idle time (20%→10%)
$500K downtime prevented
99% continuity at peak load
0 new headcount required
KPI: asset throughput · idle % · escalation response
Mavenir Systems  ·  T-Mobile Digits  ·  2019–2024
Shifted 1M-user platform from shipping-by-calendar to shipping-by-readiness
Product Lifecycle
The problem

"The release calendar was the product strategy. Nobody was asking whether the platform was actually ready to ship — they were asking whether the date was approaching. That's a dangerous place for a million-user communication platform to be."

What I found

12-week release cycles driven by calendar pressure, not readiness signals. Incident risk rising. SLA exposure growing. Engineering, sales, and ops were each solving a different version of the same problem with no shared frame for trade-offs. API contract misalignment creating silent failures downstream.

What I built

Defined a small set of operational metrics to surface failure modes early. Built AAARRR funnels and observability standards. Owned API contracts and rollout sequencing. Made explicit scope trade-offs during peak demand. Enabled 30% incremental revenue through simplified platform contracts for enterprise and SMB.

−25% release cycle (12→9 wks)
−15% downtime
$2M+ SLA exposure avoided
+30% incremental revenue
KPI: HEART · AAARRR · MTTR · SLA compliance
Mavenir Systems  ·  Rakuten Link  ·  2017–2021
Cloud-native UCaaS at scale — millions of sessions, zero tolerance for failure
Platform Engineering
The problem

"Programs were shipping without clear acceptance criteria. We'd discover problems during customer validation — the most expensive place to find them. Late-stage churn is what happens when nobody owns the definition of done."

What I found

Delivery decisions lacking clear acceptance criteria. Late-stage churn during customer validation. No telemetry on production systems. Integration failures increasing as platform scaled. Each team optimizing locally, nobody owning the end-to-end behaviour.

What I built

Led Kubernetes microservices architecture scaling millions of sessions. Implemented telemetry and alerting that transformed reactive firefighting into proactive management. Enforced backward compatibility, phased rollouts, and SOPs that survived three years in production. 99.99% uptime via MLOps + human-in-the-loop.

99.99% uptime
−20% integration failures
−20% support tickets
+30% freed engineering capacity

How I think

The stuff that doesn't
fit on a résumé.

01

"The data was already there. Someone just had to care enough to look at it."

At YVR, Maximo had the signals. At Mavenir, the production logs had the failure patterns. I didn't build new instrumentation first — I understood what existed, then decided what was missing.

02

"Most dashboards tell you what happened. I build the ones that tell you what to do next."

A metric that tells you idle time is high is not a product. A role-specific signal that tells an operator which asset to move, and when, is a product. The gap between them is the actual PM work.

03

"If you're shipping features nobody uses, you don't have a product problem. You have a listening problem."

I spent time on the airside before writing a spec. I interviewed T-Mobile ops teams before defining metrics. First principles means starting with the person doing the job, not the job description.

04

"The network already knew. Nobody was asking the right question."

SIM swaps, cell tower location, phone number mismatches — carriers have all of it. Canadian developers couldn't access it. I built the sandbox so they could. Sometimes the product is just removing the obstacle.

TelcoEdge  ·  Python / O-RAN

WeatherRAN

Rural Canadian 5G drops calls in bad weather because the tower waits for the signal to fail, then reacts. WeatherRAN reads the forecast and adapts before the rain hits. Sovereign data only — Environment Canada, no API key, no US endpoints. Built against 3GPP TR 38.901, O-RAN E2SM-RC, ITU-R specs.

AnomEdge

Three-tier AI with graceful degradation: deep learning → statistical ML → expert rules. It never fails silently. Plain-English driver alerts, not engine codes.
