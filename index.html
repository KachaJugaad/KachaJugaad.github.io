Product Lifecycle & Release Governance
The problem, in plain language

"The release calendar was the product strategy. Nobody was asking whether the platform was ready — they were asking whether the date was approaching. On a million-user platform, that's a dangerous way to make decisions."

What I found

12-week cycles driven by dates not readiness signals. SLA exposure rising. Engineering, sales, and ops solving three versions of the same problem with no shared frame. API contract misalignment creating silent downstream failures.

What I built

Operational metrics to surface failure modes early. AAARRR funnels and observability standards. API contract ownership. Explicit scope trade-offs at peak demand. Simplified platform contracts that unlocked 30% incremental revenue without adding operational load.

How I think

The stuff that doesn't
fit on a résumé.

01

"The data was already there. Someone just had to care enough to look at it."

At YVR, Maximo had the signals. At Mavenir, the production logs had the failure patterns. I didn't build new instrumentation first — I understood what existed, then decided what was actually missing.

02

"Most dashboards tell you what happened. I build the ones that tell you what to do next."

A metric reporting high idle time is not a product. A signal telling an operator which asset to move and when is a product. The gap between them is the actual PM work. I live in that gap.

03

"If you're shipping features nobody uses, you don't have a product problem. You have a listening problem."

I spent time airside before writing the YVR spec. I interviewed T-Mobile ops teams before defining metrics. First principles means starting with the person doing the job — not the job description.

04

"The network already knew. Nobody was asking it the right question."

Carriers have SIM swap data, location signals, number verification — Canadian developers couldn't access any of it. I built the sandbox because the procurement process was the product problem. Sometimes the job is removing the obstacle.
